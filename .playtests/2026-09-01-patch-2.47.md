# Softfall 2.47 playtest: reflection, feedback, priorities

Played on build 2.47 in the browser, driven through the game's own keyboard and mouse listeners.
Practice tool first (dummy, rack, an EASY archery round, the stock parkour lap), then a full
singleplayer match on seed 42 as a WARRIOR that ended in VICTORY at 10:03.

## Reflection

The presentation is well ahead of the balance. The title, class select, gear pop-up, eagle drop,
drop brief, parchment map, scoreboard and victory screen are all cohesive and readable, and the
world sells "cozy" instantly. The ideas are also all there: the built weapon, the finite quiver,
momentum on ice, the eagle as a living objective, the Keep as the respawn stake.

What the match actually plays like is different. Gold comes out of trees so fast that every
number downstream of it stops meaning anything: every bot is level 12 inside two minutes, the
forest around each Keep is a grid of a thousand stumps by minute five, and a fresh human is
fighting bots with +54 hp and +9 damage on every shot. At the same time nothing pushes the match
to a conclusion: the bot ladder has no rung for the rival eagle or Keep, so both birds sat on
full nerve for ten minutes until I walked to one and chopped it off its perch in forty seconds.
The match is simultaneously unwinnable by fighting and trivial to win by walking.

The practice tool is a good instrument with the wrong ammo: the archery round is timed on a
30-second clock while the quiver hands out one arrow every 2.4 seconds, so most of a round is
spent standing dry.

## Player feedback

Things that felt great:
- The eagle ride and the drop brief. Seeing both birds cross, the dotted line on the snow, then
  YOUR EAGLE / THEIR EAGLE is the clearest win-condition teach I have seen without a tutorial.
- Chopping. The shake, the burst, the tree becoming a stump, the +1 floaters. It feels good,
  which is exactly why everyone does it forever.
- The ability wells, the buy plates and the tooltips. Spending a point read instantly.
- The map, the scoreboard, the victory screen, the class select stage. All of it looks finished.
- The practice plank's breakable ice is a lovely secret.

Things that felt bad:
- I died in my first fight without ever seeing it: a 45 hit and a 10 and I was back at the Keep
  with an empty bag. The respawn was so quick and quiet that I only found out from the numbers.
- Ranged combat against bots is a coin flip that always lands tails. The sling stone is slow, the
  bot circles at 70 px, nothing helps you lead, and the quiver goes dry after six tries.
- Night in a stump field is a flat dark-blue nothing.
- The stump grids. Once you see them you cannot unsee them; the forest is gone and the map reads
  as a bug.
- Nothing ever came for my eagle, and nothing came for theirs. There was no reason to go
  anywhere.

## Priority list

P0: the match does not work as designed
1. Retune the chop economy. Paying 1 gold per swing at 0.34 s plus 5 per fell, with adjacent
   pines everywhere, is ~4 gold/s per player. Pay on the fell only, or make the swing pay
   fractional, or add walk time between trees (a fell cooldown, or the next pine needs clearing
   before it is choppable), and cap what a stand of forest yields before it is stumps. Retune
   `LEVEL_XP`, gear costs (10/20/35) and build costs against the new rate.
2. Give bots an objective rung. Add a ladder rung that, past some match time or when the team's
   Keep stands, sends a squad at the rival eagle, and a defend rung that answers "roost under
   attack". Until then singleplayer cannot end on its own.
3. Make the eagle a fight. 320 nerve at 10 per E swing is 11 seconds for one warrior. The gust
   should knock the swinger off the roost tiles and interrupt the swing; the bird should preen
   faster; the E swing on the roost should pay less than the arrow, or the roost should need
   two players' worth of pressure.
4. Flatten the level snowball. +6 hp and +1 dmg a level is fine at 385 gold to cap over twenty
   minutes; at two minutes it is a wall. Fix 1 mostly fixes this, but halve `LVL_DMG` growth or
   make it a percentage so a level lead never one-shots half a health bar.

P1: the fight
5. Ranged hit rate versus bots. Either the bot circle is too perfect, or the human needs help:
   a faster base projectile, a slightly bigger body hit radius for player targets, or a lead
   indicator on the aim line when the reticle is on a moving unit.
6. The quiver as the whole rhythm. Six shots then a 2.4 s trickle stalls every fight into a
   melee. Consider a larger quiver for the warrior's sling, or a faster fletch while standing
   still, and put a numeral on the reticle so a dry bow is never a surprise.
7. A death beat. A flash, a slow pull, a second of black. The `respawning` overlay may be
   there, but from the player's seat it registered as nothing.
8. Night readability on open ground: lift the floor of the night grade on snow and stumps, or
   give stumps a snow cap so they read as pale in the dark.

P2: the practice tool
9. The dummy is not a unit, so NET SHOT, BULL RUSH's carry, the crater and JUGGERNAUT's bowl
   do nothing to it. Half the kit cannot be practised. Make the dummy a unit (or route
   `hitDummy` off `hurtUnit`).
10. Nothing in the field grapples. Plant one rock and one pine inside the ring near the rack.
11. The archery round should not be quiver-gated. Fill the quiver on the ring and refill on
    every hit, or give the round its own fletch rate; the round should measure aim.
12. The parkour start box. The flag on the walk row caps the line's east end, so you enter from
    the row above and can skirt the box without arming the clock. Extend the box a tile onto the
    walk, or arm it on the first ice tile after the gate.

## Quality of life I would like to see

- A quiver numeral. On the reticle or on the weapon well: the red rim only says "no".
- Damage numbers on hits (the floater exists; make it bigger on the shot that lands).
- A "roost under attack" alert: a pulse on the minimap and the roost mark, in team colour.
- Roost and Keep icons in the map legend, and both eagles' nerve bars on the TAB scoreboard.
- A key to swing the camera to your own roost and back.
- Hold E and walk: auto-advance to the next adjacent tree so chopping is not a tap dance.
- Q or F with nothing to eat should deny (sound, well shake) instead of doing nothing.
- The event feed at 2:00 is a wall of REACHED LEVEL lines; log level 5, 10 and 12 only.
- Practice: targets on the minimap during a round, the BEST/LAST plates pinned to the HUD while
  a lap or round is live, and a ghost of the best lap on the parkour.
- Practice: reset the dummy meter with a key, and let the rack keep the class's starting bits.
- The LEAVE PRACTICE plank hangs over the HUD strip; drop it below the slab instead.
- Tooltips still carry "PRESS 1 OR CLICK TO CAST"; the key digit on the well already says it.
- Stump regrowth (a sapling after a day) so the forest is a renewable field, not a scar.
- A hint that the practice plank's ice is breakable (a hairline crack at rest) for people who
  do not click frozen things.

## Found while verifying the report commit

- A window resize that lands while the scripts are still loading throws
  `ReferenceError: fitFlakes is not defined` from canvas.js's resize listener: canvas.js loads
  before sim.js, which defines it. Seen in the browser pane, which resizes the canvas during
  load. No visible consequence was confirmed (a frozen title seen at the same time turned out
  to be the pane being hidden, which stops requestAnimationFrame). Cheap fix: guard the
  listener on boot being finished, or hoist the definition.
